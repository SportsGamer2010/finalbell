Final Bell is a gameplay-first boxing simulation. Skill-based fighting, fighter development and championship competition are primary; promotion management is secondary. Single-player must be complete without online services. Traditional and VR use common simulation contracts but have independent competitive universes, rankings, records and championship histories.

1. Product vision and design pillars 

Positioning: a skill-based boxing game with deep career consequences in a living, generational boxing ecosystem. Players can immediately fight, learn through training, progress through official titles, or run long-form single-player careers. Autonomous simulation makes the world credible without forcing administrative micromanagement.

Combat first 

Responsive, legible, tactical boxing with defense, footwork, range and stamina as first-class systems 

Single-player parity

2. Modes, identity and player journeys 

2.1 Player identity 

Default identity: player account controls a boxing stable, with optional “My Boxer” focused career onboarding. The player may directly fight as any eligible stable fighter; promotions remain an optional lightweight management layer. A fighter is an autonomous career entity and cannot be owned permanently; contracts grant representation and scheduling rights for a term.

2.3 Player journeys 

First 10 minutes: tutorial guard, jab, cross, movement and first exhibition; no promotion setup required. 

First 2 hours: create or select fighter; learn counters, distance and stamina; complete first sanctioned bout. 

First 10 hours: form rivalry, choose training camp, compete for local/state recognition; discover evolving AI contenders.

3. World topology, clocks and persistence 

3.1 Domain boundaries 

Hierarchy: Platform → Competition Domain → Universe → Organization/Promotion → Fighter. Initial domains: TRADITIONAL and VR. Domain-specific official entities have immutable domain_id; fighter IDs, titles, ranking lists, official records and career events cannot migrate across domains. Accounts and approved cosmetics may span domains. A local single-player universe is not an official online universe.

3.3 Time semantics 

Represent world_time as an integer simulation timestamp (UTC-equivalent epoch plus calendar policy). Official universes advance under a server scheduler; private offline universes advance by user action or adjustable speed. Never age the same canonical fighter twice because two linked competitions advance independently. Only one canonical owner can commit a fighter career transition at a time. All contracts, training, recovery, mandatory deadlines and fights resolve against authoritative universe time. 

3.4 Event sourcing and snapshots 

Append immutable domain events: FighterCreated, TrainingCompleted, InjuryRecorded, ContractSigned, FightScheduled, BoutStarted, RoundCompleted, FightCertified, RankingPublished, TitleAwarded, TitleVacated, FighterRetired. Materialized SQL projections support menus and leaderboards; versioned snapshots support fast load, rollback investigation and deterministic replay. Offline saves use the same event schema and local SQLite snapshots, without implying official certification. 

3.5 World simulation tick 

Read next scheduled calendar events; validate domain, universe and entity versions. 

Resolve health/recovery, training adaptation, contracts and matchmaking proposals. 

Simulate AI-versus-AI bouts with headless bout engine, record seed and engine version. 

Apply sanctioned results transactionally; update records, suspensions and mandatory obligations. 

Recompute affected ranking candidates and publish versioned snapshots on scheduled cadence. 

Generate news, rivalries and opportunities from canonical events; persist checkpoint. 

4. Fighter creation, attributes and development 

4.1 Creation and onboarding 

Character creation: name, pronouns, birthplace/residence, appearance, height, reach, stance, starting weight division, boxing background, fighting style tendencies, personality and development goals. Provide appearance presets, morph controls and optional future face-scan import with consent and moderation. Validate body proportions, reach bounds and weight eligibility. Cosmetics never change hitboxes in ranked play; canonical body geometry is constrained by competition class. 

4.2 Fighter model: true ability vs observed estimate 

FightVantage FSE infers latent capability from historical outcomes; Final Bell generates outcomes from a true internal ability state. Maintain distinct TrueAbility, ObservedScoutingEstimate, CurrentReadiness, InjuryBurden and TacticalFamiliarity. A knockout may increase public confidence in power estimates, but does not mechanically create power. Training, aging, rehabilitation and damage cause true ability changes. Coaches/scouts see imperfect estimates; official simulation sees the true state.

4.3 Development math 

Proposed: delta_ability = training_stimulus × execution_quality × coach_fit × aptitude × recovery × potential_headroom − aging_erosion − injury_loss. Temporary fight readiness is calculated separately from permanent talent. Attribute bounds use normalized 0–1 internal values, but presentation can use 0–100. Potential is multidimensional, not a single universal ceiling. No RPG XP and no direct per-win skill grant. 

Aging uses continuous attribute-specific curves; onset and magnitude are configurable. FightVantage age modifiers and late-career erosion are starting points, not production-balanced truth. Inactivity primarily affects readiness/ring rust; permanent skill erosion requires its own time-based model. Injury burden can have both acute and persistent components. 

4.4 Progression guardrails 

Finite training slots per calendar week; intensity increases fatigue and injury exposure. 

Diminishing returns by repeated drill, skill level, age and training load. 

Player minigame quality can improve adaptation but cannot bypass potential or recovery. 

No official stat farming from repeated exhibitions or duplicate/restarted online transactions. Local offline saves remain under the player’s control and never import competitive state into official online universes. 

Every update records cause, before/after values, model version and simulation timestamp. 

5. Real-time boxing combat 

5.1 Core mechanics 

Combat operates at a fixed simulation step with authoritative state: ring coordinates, stance, center of mass, guard, hands, movement, punch phase, stamina, local damage, referee state and score. Inputs express intended actions; validated animation/contact events produce outcomes. Punches: jab, cross, lead/rear hook, lead/rear uppercut, body/head variants and combinations. Defense: high/low guard, parry, slip, roll, pull, clinch, footwork and distance management. 

5.2 Punch resolution pipeline 

Capture timestamped input command with sequence and selected target. 

Check legal action, current animation state, stamina, stance and movement constraints. 

Resolve movement, hit volume and defensive collision at authoritative tick. 

Compute contact quality from geometry, timing, velocity class, balance, guard and target exposure. 

Resolve impact from fighter ability, punch type, technique, conditioning and contact quality. 

Accumulate local damage, stun, balance and knockdown probability; trigger legal referee checks. 

Publish round telemetry, scoreable events and replicated animation cues.

5.4 Skill versus fighter ability 

Player skill controls decision, timing, distance, shot selection and defensive execution; fighter stats bound feasible speed, sustained output, punch effect and technical reliability. No button mash damage multiplication. Online normalized ranked and persistent universe are distinct rulesets with clear pre-fight disclosure of stat advantages. 

5.5 Headless versus playable fights 

Use one FightRules and outcome vocabulary. Headless simulation aggregates tactical exchanges without frame-level animation, but must calibrate to playable fight outcome distributions. Do not reuse a Monte Carlo forecast as if it were a real-time combat engine. Compare KO rate, round outcomes, scoring and stamina trends across both execution paths. 

6. AI fighters and autonomous world 

6.1 In-ring AI 

Three-layer architecture: strategic game plan (style, risk, target, pace), tactical policy (read opponent, feint, counter, pressure, escape) and motor execution (legal inputs under the same fighter constraints). AI has perception delays and incomplete information. Difficulty improves decisions and consistency, not hidden health/damage buffs or superhuman reactions. 

6.2 Behavioral diversity 

Style profiles include out-boxer, pressure fighter, counterpuncher, boxer-puncher, swarmer and slugger; blend via continuous preference weights. Traits: aggression, patience, discipline, courage, adaptability, risk tolerance, coachability, loyalty, confidence and motivation. Behavior evolves with coaching, opponents, success, injury and aging. 

6.3 Agent ecology 

Autonomous promoters recruit and negotiate; managers select opportunities; trainers shape camps; sanctioning bodies enforce deadlines; fighters evaluate contracts, purses, title opportunity, opponent risk and career goals. Avoid omniscient agents: each uses an information set and bounded utility model. AI scheduling must avoid double booking and exploit loops.

8. Rankings, sanctioning and championships 

8.1 Competitive taxonomy 

Separate (a) online player skill rating, (b) fighter competitive ranking, and (c) sanctioning-body title eligibility. They answer different questions and must not be conflated. Ranking is division-, domain-, universe-, organization- and date-specific. Official fights affect canonical career records; exhibitions do not. 

8.2 Geographic and organizational belts 

Configurable ladder: local/city, state or province, multi-state regional, national, continental/intercontinental, world, unified and undisputed. Geographic eligibility is defined by fighter residency/citizenship/registration policy per organization; exceptions are explicit. State titles are not automatic prerequisites for world rankings. Each sanctioning body has its own eligible pool, rankings, mandatory rules and title lineage. 

8.3 Ranking method 

Proposed transparent composite: quality-adjusted recent results, opponent strength, activity, division eligibility and championship status, with configurable recency decay and inactivity rules. Keep ranking_score distinct from skill rating and public scouting estimates. Update after certified bouts or scheduled batch, record model version, input fight IDs and explanation. Never use future career data to rank a historical date. 

8.4 Championship lifecycle 

Publish contenders and champion with ranking snapshot. 

Identify voluntary defenses, mandatory challenger and mandatory deadline. 

Approve bout if eligibility, division weight, suspension and contract constraints pass. 

Reserve belt and sanctioning authority transactionally; prevent double title booking. 

Certify result and update title lineage, champion, rankings and mandatory queue. 

Vacate/strip on retirement, division exit, missed deadline, failed weight or disciplinary rule; record reason. 

8.5 Special cases 

Support draws, technical decisions, no-contests, disputed outcomes, failed weight, interim champions, eliminators, rematches, title unification and split organizational belts. Undisputed is derived from possession of all configured major world belts in the same division at the same timestamp. Belt naming and likenesses require licensing or fictional equivalents. 

9. Promotion and economy 

Promotion is a supporting layer: stable slots, contracts, coaches, facilities, matchmaking proposals, purses, reputation, event economics and sponsor opportunities. Player can automate most decisions. Fighter motivation considers purse, career trajectory, opposition, championship access, trainer fit, promotional reputation and personal goals. Contracts have term, fights owed, purse share, options and termination; do not model fighter as permanent property. AI competitors can sign prospects and challenge the player. Economy must avoid pay-to-win boosts and inflation exploits. 

10. Multiplayer and competitive integrity 

10.1 Official authority 

Dedicated authoritative match servers own combat truth. Client sends validated inputs, predicts locally for responsiveness and reconciles to server. Backend owns scheduling, eligibility, result certification and career transactions. Use rate limiting, anti-tamper, input sequence validation, replay-based review and idempotent result processing. 

10.2 Match flow 

Queue by domain, region, latency, skill rating, division and ruleset. 

Reserve eligible fighters; capture immutable pre-fight snapshot and ruleset version. 

Allocate match server and issue scoped join tokens. 

Run bout; log authoritative tick/event trace, disconnects and referee events. 

Sign result envelope and submit once using match_id idempotency key. 

Certify result transactionally; update fighter record, rating and title projections. 

Publish results and expose dispute/appeal workflow. 

10.3 Ranked design 

Ranked traditional gameplay is separate from VR. Within traditional, PC/console crossplay is a future policy decision dependent on measured fairness. Support normalized ranked classes for low-grind entry and a persistent career ruleset with constrained matchmaking. Never let custom offline stats enter official ranked. No gameplay advantage from purchases. 

10.4 Failure modes 

Disconnect policy distinguishes voluntary forfeit, network interruption and server failure; server crash produces void/replayable match rather than fabricated loss. Result certification is idempotent. Title reservation expires safely. All official state changes use optimistic version checks and database transactions.

13.1 Supplied code-level findings — independent verification pending 

FSE defines ten normalized attributes; baseline 0.50 and attribute-specific decay/smoothing constants. 

Smoothing uses EWMA: alpha × raw_new + (1−alpha) × previous snapshot; activity alpha is notably high (0.95). 

Age engine is side-effect free; provides phase labels, age modifiers, age–damage interactions and late-career erosion. 

Monte Carlo engine is round-by-round and path dependent, modeling stamina drain, cumulative damage, knockdowns, momentum, scorecards and stoppage probabilities. 

Monte Carlo API loads FSE snapshots and comparison metrics from Supabase; the engine itself is the stronger reuse target. 

Historical opponent context requires an as-of review: current boxer_analytics values may leak future knowledge into historical rebuilds.

14. VR expansion architecture 

VR is a separate competition domain with its own world population, records, ratings, titles, rankings, official servers and leaderboards. It reuses domain schemas, career simulation, ranking logic and combat outcomes, but uses distinct motion input, first-person presentation, comfort, safety and tuning. No official VR-versus-controller fights and no cross-domain transfer of fighter statistics or achievements. 

OpenXR-based input adapter validates punch path, velocity, guard and tracked reach. Prevent wrist-flick exploits; calibrate room boundary, standing/seated options, handedness and reach; support fatigue and comfort accessibility. Target platform-specific rendering and refresh budgets after headset selection. Shared account/cosmetic identity is permissible only where competitive-neutral. 

15. Security, operations and analytics 

Authentication: OIDC; scoped match tokens; separate local offline saves from official authority. 

Data privacy: minimize PII; consent and deletion workflows for face scans and voice; age-appropriate moderation. 

Abuse: profanity/identity moderation, reporting, match manipulation detection and anti-cheat telemetry. 

Operational SLO proposals: API p95 under 250 ms for read endpoints; match input latency budget established via prototype; certification under 10 seconds after match end. 

Observability: per-match tick overruns, packet loss, reconciliation rate, desync hashes, worker backlog, event lag, rankings freshness, crash-free sessions. 

Disaster recovery: Postgres PITR, daily backup restore drill, immutable result logs, snapshot integrity checks. 

Content versioning: ruleset, fighter model, combat engine, AI policy and schema versions pinned to official match.

16.1 Two-person responsibilities and outsourced asset decisions

17. Testing, balancing and acceptance criteria 

17.1 Automated tests 

Deterministic golden tests: same seed, ruleset and pre-fight state reproduce identical headless outcomes. 

Property tests: attributes stay bounded; no negative stamina; no double-booked official fighters; no dual title holder for exclusive belt. 

Domain isolation: all writes reject mismatched competition_domain_id, including nested foreign keys. 

Replay and idempotency: duplicate match result cannot duplicate records, money, belts or rating changes. 

Temporal integrity: rankings and scouting queries at time T cannot access records after T. 

Cross-language parity: Python reference vs C++ numerical logic within documented tolerances. 

Save compatibility: old offline worlds migrate safely or fail with explicit recovery path. 

17.2 Playtest and statistical validation 

Track hit quality, jab utility, defensive counter success, ring-cutting efficacy, punch spam, stamina curves, KO/TKO distributions, scorecard agreement, style diversity and player retention. Evaluate novice versus expert skill separation without allowing statistical attribute gaps to overwhelm skill. Calibrate AI headless outcomes against instrumented playable matches by matched cohorts.

24.1 Example domain constraint (PostgreSQL) 

Design all canonical foreign keys with domain/universe scope, not only fighter UUID.
